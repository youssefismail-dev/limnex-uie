## 2. The detectability tolerance

**The rule.**
- Take a presence outcome (a URL has a path, a query, a fragment, a root slash) with
  real rate p on the population a registration names.
- A generated rate q meets the tolerance when

      |q − p| ≤ T,   T = 2 · sqrt( p (1 − p) / n )

- n is the number of URLs of that population in one gate generation.
  - For a per-URL rate, n = 8,000: one gate generation (G = 5 × 8,000 per class).
  - For a conditional rate, n = 8,000 × the real share of the conditioning
    population. For example, the root slash among URLs with no path body.
- T is two standard errors of a rate measured on n URLs. It is the smallest
  difference a detector could see at the gate's own scale.

**Why this form** (the owner's reason, 2026-09-29).
- "3 points more" of paths was judged too much: presence rates should match the
  data's.
- A fixed number of points is meaningless for rare outcomes. ±3 points on a 1.7%
  outcome allows anything from 0% to 4.7%.
- A fixed relative band is not measurable for them. At 40,000 URLs a 1.7% rate
  carries about ±7.5% relative uncertainty.
- Two standard errors at the gate's scale is strict where a detector can see a
  difference and looser only where it cannot.

**The tolerance at the rates now registered** (K9b's shipped-weighted,
domain-frame rates, all `name_train` rows):

| outcome | real p | n | T |
|---|---|---|---|
| benign path | 73.37% | 8,000 | ±0.99 points |
| phishing path | 39.51% | 8,000 | ±1.09 points |
| benign query | 6.62% | 8,000 | ±0.56 points |
| phishing query | 4.68% | 8,000 | ±0.47 points |
| a 3% outcome | 3% | 8,000 | ±0.38 points |
| a 1% outcome | 1% | 8,000 | ±0.22 points |
| benign root slash on bare hosts (conditional) | 97.9% | about 2,000 | about ±0.64 points |
| phishing root slash on bare hosts (conditional) | 99.9% | about 4,700 | about ±0.09 points |

The slash rows are illustrations. E11's registration fixes their populations and
n exactly.

**How a registration reads it.**
- **The data.** The real rate p is measured on all real rows of the named
  population, not only rows that fit the model. A detector compares with real URLs.
  - For today's rates the difference is inside T: path 73.37% against 73.3% on rows
    that fit.
- **The generated rate.** q is one training's point estimate from its registered
  generation, normally 40,000 URLs per class. Its own standard error is under half
  of T.
- **PASS** if |q − p| ≤ T; **FAIL** otherwise.
- **Near the edge.** One training varies from the next.
  - A FAIL within the recipe's measured seed-to-seed spread S of the edge
    (T < |q − p| ≤ T + S) is flagged in the results.
  - The registration names S and its source.
  - The owner may order a replicate training with a new registered seed. The
    decision then uses the mean of the two trainings.
  - A flag never turns a FAIL into a PASS by itself.
- **Scope.**
  - free mode: against the shipped-weighted real rate, as K9b;
  - control mode: per state;
  - per class, always.

**What it does not change:**
- K9b's and K10a–K10b's registered bands, which are read as registered;
- gate v2, its thresholds, E5 and R4;
- content realism, which stays the gate's job.

**For the record, not a re-grading.** On this standard the baseline's free-mode
presence (`e9_ln` / raw, `E9_RESULTS.md`) would not meet the tolerance:

| class | path, generated against real | query, generated against real |
|---|---|---|
| benign | 75.58% against 73.37%: +2.21 points, outside ±0.99 | 4.58% against 6.62%: outside ±0.56 |
| phishing | 38.20% against 39.51%: −1.31 points, outside ±1.09 | 2.95% against 4.68%: outside ±0.47 |

## 3. The finish line for E3

- **The rule.** E3 is claimed only when two conditions hold:
  1. every gated cell is CLEARED (`NATIVE_REPAIR_PLAN.md` N3, unchanged);
  2. the root slash on URLs with no path body meets the detectability tolerance
     (§2), in both classes, in free mode and in control mode's path-off states.
     It covers two kinds of URL:
     - bare hosts: real rates 97.9% benign and 99.9% phishing;
     - host-route queries: 99.6% and 100%.
     Both rates are from `NATIVE_REPAIR_PLAN.md` K3.
- **Why** (the owner). The tokenizer cannot write the slash, so E7-type models write
  it 0% of the time. A whole-URL detector would catch that at once.
  - It is FULL's largest term. Rendering the slash alone takes E7's FULL from
    +0.2168 to +0.0720 (benign) and from +0.4018 to +0.0520 (phishing)
    (`NATIVE_REPAIR_PLAN.md` §5.3).
- **The gate is unchanged.** FULL stays auxiliary under gate v2, as registered.
  Condition 2 is an owner condition on claiming E3, not a new gate cell. FULL is
  reported with every E3 reading.

## 4. Capacity (R3)

- **R3 stays LOCKED.** Capacity is the last resort. It is considered only in one of
  two cases:
  1. the architectural, objective and representational candidates for the failing
     cells have each been tested or ruled out by a registered reading;
  2. there is strong proof: a registered reading, on the current baseline, that
     capacity is the binding constraint.
- **What counts as strong proof.** N2a's registered unlock readings
  (FREQUENCY-SPECIFIC or GENERAL, `NATIVE_REPAIR_PLAN.md` §6, N2a) re-measured on
  `e9_ln`'s objective count. N2a itself read SPARSITY on the old objective, which
  does not unlock R3.
- Any capacity probe needs the owner's approval.
- R2 (more steps) stays REJECTED.

## 5. E11's scope

- E11 combines two things in one experiment:
  - the redesign of how presence is decided: hidden controls, the plan, or a
    supplied plan;
  - the root slash (`<RS>`, the tokenizer flag written for E8; E8's results stay
    unread).
- **Why together.** The slash adds a decision at the end of the host. That is where
  the query-start deficit is worst (QG1's host route: 0.539 / 0.592). Tuning
  presence on a sequence structure E11 would then change is wasted work.
- **Order.**
  1. E10_generate's registered readout;
  2. PC1;
  3. E11's registration, drafted in `E11_ROADMAP.md`;
  4. code;
  5. the owner runs training and generation;
  6. the laptop readout.
- E11 targets structure, not content. Query and path content (the convicted
  control cells) are E12's question.
