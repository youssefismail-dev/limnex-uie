## 4. Copying — the owner's question, answered

**The question.** When the analysis says the model copies training data less than
real URLs do, what exactly is measured?

### 4.1 The definition

The registered metric is K11d's "copied queries" (`research/native/e11_score.py`,
part `misc`). E9, E10 and DC1 used the same one.

| | definition |
|---|---|
| scope | **the query component only:** `FIELDS["QRY"](url)`, the parsed query, the text after `?` and before `#`, without the `?`. `https://a.com/p?x=1&y=2#frag` gives `x=1&y=2`; `https://a.com/` gives nothing |
| match | exact equality of the **whole** query string with some query string among the class's training URLs, `name_train` and `structure_train` (`dc1_compare.training_queries`), checked against the URL's own class. Not substrings, not parameters one by one |
| numerator | generated URLs whose query matches |
| denominator | generated URLs with a non-empty query |
| pooling in the reports | per arm and mode (free; control = all 8 states), **summed over the two classes** |

- **Complete URLs are a separate metric:** "novel" in the same table.
  - It counts whole URLs, normalised by `normalize_for_dedup`, absent from the
    Bloom filter of known URLs.
  - The numerator is novel URLs; the denominator is all generated URLs.
- **So the copying metric is query-only.** It is not about complete URLs, other
  fields, substrings or a mixture.

### 4.2 The four checks

**1. Query-only — PASS.** See §4.1.

**2. The reference uses the same definition — PASS on the definition; the
populations are not yet matched.**
- The natural rate (`e12_sizing.py`) uses the same extractor, the same training
  set per class, the same exact whole-string match and the same denominator rule.
  Its population is the gate's free-mode QRY pool: `name_val`, one URL per host,
  the gate's seed.
- **Not yet matched:**
  - the registered generated numbers sum both classes and all control states;
  - the reference is per class, on the free-mode pool.
- **The one matched comparison available now:** `e11_plan`, free mode, pooled.
  - Generated: 19.47% (`E11_STEP20K_RESULTS.md` §6).
  - Expected: 34.5%, the per-class natural rates weighted by `e11_plan`'s own
    free-mode query counts (2,628 benign, 1,868 phishing).
  - With phishing's conservative reference (check 3), 28.1%. The generated rate
    is below both.
- **Still to measure:** per class, and per control state against each state's own
  real pool. That is QC1-1.

**3. Leakage-safe, with the right train/validation relationship — PASS for benign;
phishing needs two references.**
- **Benign.** The split is by registrable domain (`CORPUS_AUDIT.md`: "disjointness
  is a property of the data structure"). Verified here: all 7,253 pool URLs are on
  registrable domains that occur nowhere in benign training. Every natural match
  is reuse between different sites.
- **Phishing.** The split is by **host**, by design (`merge_phishing.py`). 73% of
  phishing sits on shared hosting, where the attacker's name is the subdomain.
  - So a held-out phishing host can share a registrable domain with a training
    host: a shared platform, or the same attacker's domain.
  - This is the case for 596 of the 1,068 pool hosts.
  - On the fully domain-disjoint subset (472 URLs) the natural rate is **26.5%**,
    against **41.9%** at the host level.
- **Which reference is right depends on the unit.**
  - The gate compares one real URL per host, and the generator invents hosts,
    shared platforms included. So the host-level rate is the gate-consistent
    reference.
  - The domain-disjoint rate is its conservative bound.
  - Both are reported; neither is dropped.
- **Nothing on either side trains on the validation data.** `final.pt` involves no
  selection on it at all.

**4. Moving toward the real rate is justified — PASS, with a refinement.**
- **Why it is justified.** A generator that samples the real distribution copies at
  the natural rate. A held-out real URL is itself such a sample, compared with
  the same training set.
  - A rate well below the natural one therefore means the common strings are
    under-produced.
  - Moving toward it is movement toward the real distribution, which is what the
    gate tests.
- **Why the rate alone is not enough:**
  - it can be matched with the wrong strings;
  - memorisation shows as copying of **rare** training strings.
- **Real data copies rare strings too.** Matches to strings seen exactly once in
  training are 4.6% (benign) and 5.1% (phishing) of the real pool:

  | natural matches, as share of the pool | benign | phishing |
  |---|---|---|
  | string seen 20+ times in training | 13.1% | 26.3% |
  | seen 5–19 times | 6.9% | 5.1% |
  | seen 2–4 times | 4.6% | 5.3% |
  | seen once | 4.6% | 5.1% |
  | **all** | **29.3%** | **41.9%** |

- **So the guard is refined, not removed.**

### 4.3 The treatment in E12 — conditional on the owner's review

- **E11 is not reinterpreted.** K11d stays as registered, and `e11_plan` fails it.
- **Proposed for E12,** fixed in E12's registration once QC1-1 has measured the
  matched references:
  - **Overall copying** is compared two-sided with the natural rate of the same
    class, mode and state, within the detectability tolerance. Lower is no longer
    read as better in itself.
  - **The memorisation guard** is one-sided. Copying of strings seen once in
    training must not exceed the natural rate of the same by more than the
    tolerance.
  - The match-band profile is reported.
  - Phishing is read against both references (§4.2, check 3).
